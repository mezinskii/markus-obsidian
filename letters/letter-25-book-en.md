Hello,

For the past year I have been working on a book, and today it is out on Kindle. It is called *Reading Marcus Aurelius: The Meditations as Spiritual Exercises*.

Many readers meet the *Meditations* the way I first did: a few sentences stay with you, but the book as a whole seems a scattering of notes that keep repeating the same few things. What changed this for me was a different question. Not "what is wrong with this text?" but "why did Marcus write it at all?" He wrote for himself. His notes were exercises, and they make sense once they are read as exercises.

What is inside:

- the complete *Meditations* in George Long's translation, book by book;
- commentary on 107 key passages: what the Greek says, where the translation loses something, and how each passage connects with the rest of the book;
- for each of the twelve books, its key thought and one exercise Marcus practiced himself;
- a map of practice: the Stoic doctrines and exercises, with every passage where each appears.

Much of what you have read in these letters went into the book, reworked and checked against the Greek. If the letters have been useful to you, the book is the same work, gathered in one place and taken further.
