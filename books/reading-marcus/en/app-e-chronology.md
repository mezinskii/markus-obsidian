Only the main landmarks of Marcus Aurelius's life are gathered here, those needed to understand the notes "Among the Quadi at the Granua" and "This in Carnuntum" and the mentions of people close to him in the text. Dates follow the generally accepted chronology (above all Anthony Birley's biography of Marcus); where sources differ or a date can only be reconstructed approximately, this is marked with "c." or "according to other sources." Links lead to the passages where a landmark is echoed in the *Meditations*.

- **121**: On April 26 Marcus Annius Verus, the future Marcus Aurelius, is born in Rome.
- **c. 124**: His father, the praetor Marcus Annius Verus, dies. The boy grows up in the house of his grandfather, also Marcus Annius Verus; thanks to his grandfather and father open the *Meditations* ([[01-01|1.1]], [[01-02|1.2]]). Marcus's parents may also be meant in [[08-25|8.25]]: "Lucilla saw Verus die, and then Lucilla died."
- **138**: In February Hadrian adopts Antoninus on condition that he adopt Marcus and Lucius Verus. In July Hadrian dies and Antoninus becomes emperor. To Antoninus Marcus devotes the longest portrait of Book I ([[01-16|1.16]]) and another in [[06-30|6.30]]; Hadrian and Antoninus are named together among forgotten names in [[04-33|4.33]] and [[08-25|8.25]], and the court of Hadrian is recalled again in [[10-27|10.27]].
- **139**: Marcus receives the title of Caesar and moves into the palace.
- **140s**: Studies with Fronto, begun in the late 130s ([[01-11|1.11]]), and the turn to philosophy. Rusticus lends Marcus the records of Epictetus's discourses ([[01-07|1.7]]); the exact date is unknown.
- **145**: Marriage to Faustina the Younger, daughter of Antoninus. In [[01-17|1.17]] Marcus thanks the gods "that I have such a wife, so obedient, and so affectionate, and so simple."
- **147**: Birth of the first child; Marcus receives the tribunician power (*tribunicia potestas*), the powers of a tribune of the people without the office itself. It was one of the chief powers of an emperor, and with it Marcus becomes the junior colleague of Antoninus.
- **c. 155–161**: Death of his mother, Domitia Lucilla (the exact date is unknown). In [[01-17|1.17]] Marcus thanks the gods "that, though it was my mother's fate to die young, she spent the last years of her life with me."
- **161**: On March 7 Antoninus dies. Marcus becomes emperor and makes Lucius Verus his co-emperor. In the same year Commodus is born.
- **162–166**: War with Parthia; the army is nominally led by Lucius Verus.
- **165–166**: Troops returning from the East bring an epidemic (the "Antonine plague"), which does not abate until the end of Marcus's reign. Perhaps it is echoed in [[09-02|9.2]], where Marcus compares the corruption of the mind with a "corruption and change of this atmosphere."
- **c. 167–168**: Germanic tribes cross the Danube; the Marcomannic Wars begin. In 168 both emperors set out for the north.
- **169**: Early in the year, on the way back from the north, Lucius Verus dies. He and those who sat by his tomb are spoken of in [[08-37|8.37]]; thanks for such a brother stand in [[01-17|1.17]].
- **c. 171–173**: Marcus's main headquarters at Carnuntum, a Roman camp on the Danube. The note "This in Carnuntum" stands in the manuscript after [[02-17|2.17]], between the second and third books; some editors take it as the heading of Book III.
- **c. 172–173**: Campaigns against the Quadi beyond the Danube. The note "Among the Quadi at the Granua" stands after [[01-17|1.17]], at the end of Book I; the dating is approximate. Perhaps Book I was written no earlier than Book II.
- **175**: The revolt of Avidius Cassius in the East; three months later Cassius is killed by his own men. Marcus travels to the East; on the way, in Cappadocia, Faustina dies (in the winter of 175/176, according to other sources in 176).
- **176**: Return to Rome; triumph together with Commodus.
- **177**: Commodus receives the title of Augustus and becomes his father's co-emperor.
- **178**: Marcus sets out with Commodus for the Danube again.
- **180**: On March 17 Marcus dies in a military camp, according to some sources at Vindobona (present-day Vienna), according to others at Sirmium. Commodus remains sole emperor.

When exactly the *Meditations* were written, the sources do not say. The two notes show that at least part of the books was written on campaign, during the wars on the Danube, that is, in the 170s.
